Discrete Mathematics Theoretical Foundations

Theorem 1: Bipartiteness of Grid Graph

Two seats $(r_1, c_1)$ and $(r_2, c_2)$ are adjacent iff $|r_1 - r_2| + |c_1 - c_2| = 1$. The parity $(r + c) \pmod 2$ strictly alternates across every edge. Thus, all closed cycles have even length. By König's Theorem, a graph has no odd cycles if and only if it is bipartite (χ(G) = 2).

Theorem 2: 4-Colourability of King's Graph

Every $2 \times 2$ block of seats forms a complete subgraph $K_4$ where all pairs share an edge. Since χ(G) ≥ ω(G) = 4, at least 4 colours are necessary. The 2D modular formula $\text{colour}(r, c) = (r \bmod 2) \times 2 + (c \bmod 2)$ guarantees adjacent seats never share coordinates in modulo 2, achieving a proper 4-colouring.

Theorem 3: Pigeonhole Feasibility Bound

To achieve zero conflicts, every student of course $C$ must sit in an independent set. If $|C| > \alpha(G_{\text{seat}})$, by the Pigeonhole Principle, at least two students must occupy adjacent seats, rendering zero conflicts mathematically impossible.

Theorem 4: Greedy Colouring Bound Δ(G) + 1

A greedy vertex colouring uses at most $\Delta(G) + 1$ colours. Since a vertex has at most $\Delta(G)$ neighbours, at least one colour from $\{0, 1, \dots, \Delta(G)\}$ remains free.
